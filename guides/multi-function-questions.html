Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Multi-function appliances sell on the functions you might use. The honest test is narrower: name the three you will use every week, and check that those three are good. The rest is storage, controls and cleaning you did not ask for.

The short answer

Write the three functions you will use weekly. If the appliance is not clearly good at those three, the rest of the feature list is decoration.

Name your three functions

Air fry, roast, reheat — those are the three most people actually use. If a function is not on your list, it should not move the decision, no matter how many presets it adds to the panel.

Distinguish functions from presets

A function is a cooking mode. A preset is a saved temperature and time for one food. Twenty-four presets can still be three functions. The Emeril listing is a good example: it advertises 24 one-touch presets, and the modes behind them are air fry, bake, broil, dehydrate, roast, slow cook, toast and reheat.

Check whether accessories are included or extra

Rotisserie spits, crisper baskets and bake pans are often the difference between a function being usable and being a promise. The Emeril listing names six included components: rack, bake pan, drip tray, crisper basket, rotisserie spit, fetch tool. Its title says six accessories and its configuration field says seven — flagged as FLG-031.

Ask what breaks first

More functions means more sensors, more moving parts and more to clean. A four-preset basket fryer has almost nothing to go wrong. A 26-quart oven with a rotisserie motor has considerably more.

Check the manual before you trust a preset count

Where a listing contradicts itself — 24 presets in the title, 25 in the bullet, 400 °F in the spec field and 450 °F in the title — the manual is the tie-breaker, not the marketing copy.
