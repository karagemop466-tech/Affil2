Air fryer for a small kitchen: the three measurements that decide it

In a small kitchen the constraint is never features. It is the 13 inches between the kettle and the wall. Three measurements settle almost every decision, and none of them is capacity.

The short answer

Measure depth, measure the space in front, and weigh whether it will go in a cupboard. Pick the unit that survives all three; then look at features.

1. Depth, plus the air gap

Depth is the first disqualifier. Both Chefman basket units are under 14 inches deep; the oven-style Emeril is 16.9. Add the clearance the manual asks for behind and above, and you have your real number.

2. Door swing or drawer pull

A drawer needs room in front for you to stand and pull. French doors need room in front for two doors to open and stay open. In a narrow galley that difference decides whether the appliance is usable at all.

3. Where it lives between uses

If it goes in a cupboard, weight decides whether that happens twice or never. The 8-quart Chefman is 12.8 pounds and the 6-quart dual-basket Chefman is 13.3 pounds, both realistically movable. The Emeril listing does not state an item weight (FLG-022), and its 16.9-inch depth already rules out tight counters and most standard cupboards.

What to give up first

Windows, presets, and a second basket are all things you can live without. Depth and weight are not. If the counter is tight, a single-basket unit with no window is the honest choice, and it is also the cheapest of the three here.
