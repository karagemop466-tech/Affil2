Air fryer for a small kitchen: the three measurements that decide it

In a small kitchen, measure the actual counter or cupboard space first. Check the appliance's depth, how it opens, and where it will be stored before comparing feature lists.

The short answer

Measure the counter and door/drawer access, then check storage and the maker's clearance instructions. Compare features only after the physical fit is clear.

1. Depth, plus the air gap

Depth is the first disqualifier. Both Chefman basket units are under 14 inches deep; the oven-style Emeril is 16.9. Add the clearance the manual asks for behind and above, and you have your real number.

2. Door swing or drawer pull

A drawer needs room in front for you to stand and pull. French doors need room in front for two doors to open and stay open. In a narrow galley that difference decides whether the appliance is usable at all.

3. Where it lives between uses

If you plan to put the appliance away, compare the listed weight, handling instructions, and storage space. The P-03 listing states 12.8 pounds and the P-02 listing 26.5 pounds; check those values on the current pages before buying.

Choose the trade-offs you can live with

A window, presets, and separate baskets are features to compare against your routine. If none is important to you, do not let its presence decide the purchase. Check dimensions and handling for every model you consider.
