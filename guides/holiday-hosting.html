Holiday hosting 2026: can an air fryer carry the big meal?

Nobody loses the holidays over the turkey. They lose it to oven scheduling — three sides, one oven, and guests who arrived early. Here is what an air fryer can honestly take off your plate this season, and which of the three we cover fits the job. Cooking method notes below are editorial kitchen practice — not lab results, and not claims about any product's performance; product details come from the official listings.

The short answer

Keep the centerpiece in your main oven. Hand off the sides, the appetizers, and the reheat to an air fryer: two baskets for parallel sides (P-01), one big batch for volume (P-03), or a countertop oven for trays and browning (P-02).

Three jobs worth handing off

Job 1

Sides that need crispness

Roasted potatoes, Brussels sprouts, dinner rolls for the second batch — anything you want browned while the oven is committed to the bird. Two independent baskets mean two sides at once, timed together.

Job 2

Appetizers while guests arrive

Wings, fries, spring rolls, reheated bites from the freezer. This is the hour when a second cooking station is worth more than anything else in the kitchen.

Job 3

The second-wave reheat

Leftovers and late plates. USDA guidance is to reheat leftovers to 165 °F (73.9 °C) internal temperature — measure, don't guess (USDA FSIS temperature chart).

What not to expect

  • A basket air fryer is not a turkey oven. Even 8 quarts is a side-dish machine at a holiday table.
  • "Up to 500 °F" on the P-02 listing is a ceiling, not a promise about every mode — check the live listing for what each function does.
  • We show no prices, star ratings, or "best of" rankings here. Details change; the listing is the truth at the moment you buy.

The one-page game plan

  1. Write the menu and mark which dishes need the big oven. Those stay.
  2. Everything that needs crispness or fast heat goes to the countertop appliance.
  3. Decide parallel (two baskets) vs volume (one big basket) vs tray-style (countertop oven).
  4. Measure the counter — including door swing and clearance — before ordering.
  5. Plan the reheat window; leftovers go to 165 °F per USDA.
