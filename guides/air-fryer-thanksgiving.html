Air fryer at Thanksgiving: what actually fits

Thanksgiving is the one day a year when counter space and usable volume both matter at once. The useful question is not how many quarts the appliance has — it is which of the day's jobs it can take off the oven, and which it cannot.

The short answer

Use the countertop appliance for sides and reheats, keep the bird in the oven, and check what else is on the circuit before you plug in a second 1700-watt unit.

What a countertop oven can take off your hands

Sides and reheats are the realistic wins: a tray of roasted vegetables, dinner rolls, a reheat of something that came out of the oven too early. The Emeril listing states it fits a 12-inch pizza and can rotisserie a 6-pound chicken, which is a concrete statement about usable interior size.

What it cannot

A whole turkey is not going in any of these. Be sceptical of any countertop appliance marketing that implies otherwise. The Emeril's stated rotisserie capacity is a 6-pound chicken, which is a bird, not a feast bird.

Where a basket fryer earns its counter space

Small batches that need to be hot and crisp at the moment they hit the table: sprouts, potatoes, a last-minute tray of something. The dual-basket Chefman lets you run two of those at once and finish them together, which is more useful on the day than a bigger single basket.

Check the circuit before the menu

All three air fryers here list 1700 watts in their specification tables (and two 1700-watt appliances on one kitchen circuit plus an oven is a tripped breaker, not a crispier sprout). Wattage is worth knowing before the day — which is why the 8-quart Chefman's contradictory 1700 W spec field and 1000 W bullet are flagged as FLG-017.
