The turkey thermometer game plan: instant-read, wireless, or both?

The only reliable answer to "is it done?" is a number, not a timer. Here is the temperature target that actually matters, and how the two thermometers we cover — one instant-read, one wireless — fit into the plan.

The number to know

USDA's published guidance: cook all poultry — whole birds, breasts, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing — to 165 °F (73.9 °C) internal temperature, as measured with a food thermometer. Leftovers and casseroles go back to 165 °F on reheat. Beef/pork/veal/lamb steaks and roasts: 145 °F with a 3-minute rest. Ground meats: 160 °F. These are USDA's numbers, not ours — read the full chart at fsis.usda.gov (chart last updated 2025-04-14).

Instant-read: the spot check

An instant-read is the one you reach for at the critical moment: pull it from the drawer, probe the thickest part of the thigh away from bone, read, done. The listing for the ThermoMaven states a 1-second response and ±0.5 °F accuracy, with NIST and NSF certification claimed on the page.

Game plan for the big day

  1. Decide your target from USDA's chart (poultry: 165 °F) before the cooking starts.
  2. If you only buy one: the instant-read covers the safety check for every dish.
  3. If the bird is in for hours: a wireless probe earns its hub; keep the instant-read for sides and the final check.
  4. Probe the thickest part of the thigh, away from bone, and measure leftovers at 165 °F on reheat.
