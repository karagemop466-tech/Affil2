How to assess thermometer accuracy claims

A listing's accuracy tolerance is a seller-stated claim, not independent test data. Check what range and conditions it covers, whether supporting documentation is available, and how the maker says to check or calibrate the instrument.

The short answer

Read the exact tolerance and conditions, look for the maker's supporting documentation, and use official food-safety guidance for cooking targets. We have not independently tested either model.

Separate the claim from the evidence

Read an accuracy tolerance separately from response time, resolution, and calibration. The P-04 listing states a tolerance, but this site has not independently measured it or seen a test report. The P-05 listing does not provide an equivalent result in the material recorded here.

Ask what a certification statement covers

The P-04 seller listing uses the words NIST and NSF, and states that a calibration certificate is included. We have not inspected a certificate or verified what the seller's certification wording applies to. Ask the maker for the scope and supporting documentation before treating the wording as independent proof.

Read the calibration instructions

Check the maker's instructions for calibration or verification steps, conditions, and what to do if a reading is outside tolerance. A certificate mentioned in a listing is not the same as a procedure we have independently checked.

Check resolution separately from accuracy

Do not treat the number of displayed digits or a fast response-time claim as evidence of accuracy. Compare those fields separately and check the exact conditions attached to the stated tolerance.

Use an official food-safety source

For cooking targets, use the USDA FSIS chart linked on this page. A seller's accuracy number is not a food-safety target; follow the chart and the thermometer maker's instructions for placement and use.
