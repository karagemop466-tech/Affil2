Your air fryer's first week: what to cook, what to skip

The fastest way to regret a new appliance is to feed it only frozen fries. A week of deliberate experiments teaches you where the heat sits and what this machine will actually replace in your routine. The practice below is kitchen experience, not product testing &mdash; no performance claim about any of these machines is made here.

The short answer

Calibrate with something cheap, cook your real weeknight meal by day four, and give the appliance one standing job from the oven. Skip batters and overloaded baskets until the machine is familiar.

Days one and two: learn the heat

Start with something cheap and forgiving — potato wedges or chicken wings. The point is not dinner, it is calibration: a single layer, a shake halfway, and a note on what you would change next time. You are learning how done looks at 400 degrees versus 375 on your counter, not on anyone else's.

Days three and four: cook the meal you repeat

Now cook the thing you make on busy nights — one protein and one vegetable. This is where basket layout decides the rhythm: two baskets run a main and a side to the same finish line, one big basket takes the full family portion in a single batch, and an oven-style tray takes the jobs that want a rack rather than a drawer.

What to skip for now

Wet batters — moving air strips them before they set. Overcrowded loads — stacked food steams instead of crisping, and two smaller batches beat one large one every time. High-sugar glazes until you know your hot spots, because sugar burns fast in moving air. None of this is a product claim; it is kitchen practice that holds across machines.

The habit that pays for the counter space

Once a week, let the appliance win one job from the big oven: reheating, roasting a tray of vegetables, finishing a weeknight protein. If it never wins a job, it becomes cupboard inventory. If it wins one permanently, it earned the space — and cleanup is half the ownership, which is covered in the cleanup and counter-space guide.
