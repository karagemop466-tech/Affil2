Your air fryer's first week: what to cook, what to skip

The fastest way to regret a new appliance is to feed it only frozen fries. The fastest way to love it is a week of deliberate experiments — three things to cook, two things to skip, and one habit that makes every weeknight easier after that. The cooking practice below is editorial kitchen experience, not product testing; product details come from the official Amazon listings as of 2026-11-20.

The short answer

A week of deliberate experiments: potato wedges or wings first, then the meal you actually repeat; skip wet batters and overcrowded loads for now; and make the fryer win one real job from the big oven — that habit is what earns the counter space.

Day 1–2: learn the heat

Start with something cheap and forgiving: potato wedges or chicken wings. The point is not dinner — it is calibration. Preheat the way the manual says (most listings call for a short preheat), keep the food in a single layer, and shake or flip halfway. You are learning where this machine's hot spots are and how done looks at 400 °F versus 375 °F. Note what you would change next time.

Day 3–4: cook the meal you actually repeat

Now cook the thing you make on busy nights — a protein and a vegetable. This is where basket layout decides your rhythm:

What to skip (for now)

  • Wet batters. Anything drippy needs a coating or a pan; the fan will strip batter off food before it sets.
  • Overcrowded "just one more" loads. Stacked food steams instead of crisping. Two smaller batches beat one large one — this is the most common first-week disappointment.
  • Untested high-sugar glazes. Sugar burns fast in moving air; add glaze late in the cook and watch it.

The habit that pays for the counter space

Once a week, make the appliance the default for one thing you used the big oven for: reheating pizza, roasting a tray of vegetables, finishing a weeknight protein. If it never wins a job from the oven, it becomes cupboard inventory. If it wins one job permanently, it earned the counter.

  2. Answer one question: do I cook main-and-side together (two baskets) or one big thing (one basket)?
