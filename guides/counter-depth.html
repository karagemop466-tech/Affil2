Will it fit? Measure depth, door swing and clearance before anything else

The first thing a countertop appliance has to do is sit on the counter. That sounds obvious until you measure the space between the kettle and the wall, and then measure the appliance. Depth is the measurement that disqualifies more kitchens than any other.

The short answer

Measure depth plus clearance, then the space in front, then the headroom. Buy the unit that survives all three, and treat features as a tiebreaker.

Get the depth right, including the gap behind

The three air fryers here are 16.9, 13.38 and 12.75 inches deep, as stated in their listing spec panels. Add the clearance the manual asks for behind and above the vents. A unit pushed flat against a wall is a unit recirculating its own hot air.

Door swing versus drawer pull

A basket fryer needs room in front for you to stand and pull a drawer towards you. The French-door oven needs room in front for two doors to open and stay open while you slide a rack out. In a narrow galley that difference decides whether the appliance is usable at all.

Width is the easy one; height is the trap

Width matters if the appliance has to live between two fixed objects. Height matters if it lives under a cupboard, because the top vents need somewhere to exhaust. Check the cupboard above, not just the counter below.

Where it lives between uses

If the plan is to put it away after each use, the plan has to survive a drawer pull, a door swing and a lift. This site prints a weight only when the listing showed one: 13.3 lb for the 6-quart Chefman. The other two listings did not show a weight in the text we fetched (FLG-040), so no weight is printed for them.

The two-minute test

Cut three strips of paper to 16.9, 13.38 and 12.75 inches. Lay each on the counter where the appliance would go, then stand in front of it. If the paper reaches the wall or the sink, that unit does not fit.
