How many presets do you actually need? What a preset count tells you, and what it hides

Preset counts are the cheapest number in an appliance listing to inflate and the easiest for a shopper to mistake for capability. Here is the whole argument for ignoring them, using nothing but the listings this site was built from.

What we would do

Presets are shortcuts, not abilities. Buy on maximum temperature, basket shape, usable area and shut-off behaviour. Treat the preset count as marketing copy: on one of the five listings we read, the title and the bullets do not agree on it.

01What a preset physically is

A stored pairing of a temperature and a run time, sometimes with a fan speed. That is all. A preset does not add heat, capacity or a cooking method; it saves you two button presses on a dish you make often. Nothing on a specification sheet changes because a tenth preset was added to the panel.

02Why counts are not comparable between brands

Count what, exactly? On the 8-quart Chefman the listing names four: French fries, chicken, meat, fish. On the Emeril oven the listing prints 24 one-touch presets in its title and description and 25 cooking pre-sets in the About-this-item bullets — the same page, both numbers, read on 2026-11-20. A number that cannot stay still on one listing is not a measurement you should compare across two.

03What to read instead, on the same sheet

Four lines that actually change your experience. Maximum temperature: 450 °F with a Hi-Fry option on both Chefman units, 75–500 °F as the stated range on the oven-style unit. Whether the basket has a window. Whether there is an automatic shut-off (all three listings state one). And whether the parts that touch food are described as dishwasher-safe, which is a claim about a part, not a claim about your evening.

04The two presets worth having

If you take nothing else from this page: the useful controls are the ones that fix a mistake, not the ones that name a food. A +2 minute button (stated on the dual-basket listing) and a shake reminder (the LED shake reminder on the 8-quart) answer the two things that actually go wrong in a small hot cavity: it needed longer, and it needed turned. Frozen-chicken mode does not.

05How to test a preset in your own week one

Pick the one preset closest to what you bought the appliance for. Run it exactly as the manual says, then write down two things when it beeps: the surface you wanted and whether the middle was done. That is the only comparison that means anything, because the number in your kitchen is measured against your food, not against a marketing table. We are deliberately not publishing cook times here. Nobody on this site cooked in these units, and a time copied from a listing bullet is not a test result.
