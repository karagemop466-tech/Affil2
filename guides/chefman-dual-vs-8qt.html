Chefman dual basket versus the 8-quart: the counter-space decision

Both are Chefman basket fryers running at 120 volts and 1700 watts. The decision between the 6-quart dual-basket and the 8-quart single basket is really a decision about how you cook and how much counter you are willing to give up.

The short answer

Two baskets for a main-and-a-side cook, one big basket for a single-batch cook. Measure the depth and the clearance first, then let the counter decide between the two layouts.

Same brand, different machine

The 6-quart dual-basket unit runs two 3-quart baskets, is rated 200–450 °F, and measures 13.38 inches deep, 11.81 wide and 10.63 tall, at 13.3 pounds for the 6-quart dual-basket Chefman. The 8-quart runs a single basket, is rated 200–400 °F with a 450 °F Hi-Fry option in the title, and measures 12.75 inches deep, 13.5 wide and 10 tall, at 12.8 pounds for the 8-quart Chefman.

Two baskets or one

The dual-basket is built to cook a main and a side together and finish them together, through its Sync Finish feature. The 8-quart is built to take one larger batch in a single flat basket. The choice is about your weeknight pattern, not about which number is bigger.

Measure depth and width, not just quarts

Depth is the number that usually disqualifies a counter. Both units are around 12 to 13 inches deep, but both need clearance above the vents and room in front. Write the depth and the available space down before you compare the quart figures.

Watch the listing contradictions

Each listing contradicts itself, and both are printed here rather than resolved. The 8-quart prints 1700 watts in its specs and 1000 watts in its bullets (FLG-017), and 400 °F in its specs against 450 °F Hi-Fry in its title (FLG-018). The dual-basket prints 6 quarts in its title and 6 liters in its measurements field (FLG-034).

Pick by your weeknight

If you routinely cook a main and a side at once, the dual basket does the job it is built for. If you cook one big batch at a time and want the simplest operation, the 8-quart fits. Measure the counter first, then choose the layout that matches how you actually cook.
