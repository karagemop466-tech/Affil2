Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

A useful first question is how the appliance opens: a drawer to pull, or a door to swing. That affects where it can sit and how you load it. Shape alone does not say which one will cook better for your meals.

The short answer

Measure the usable space and clearances, then compare how each model opens and what fits inside. Use a regular meal to decide whether one area, separate baskets, or listed oven accessories matter to you.

What actually differs

A basket model uses a removable drawer or basket; an oven-style model has a door and may include racks or trays. Check the exact configuration on the listing and manual. Compare the access route, removable parts, and where you will set down hot food; we have not tested cooking speed or results.

Measure the footprint, including the swing

The current records list P-02 at 16.9 inches deep, P-01 at 13.38 inches, and P-03 at 12.75 inches. Recheck those values on each live listing and use the maker's manual for clearance. Include the wall behind it, space above it, handles, and the path a door or drawer needs.

Think about how you load it, and how you clean it

A drawer and a door-and-rack layout involve different loading and unloading steps. Check which pieces are removable and how the manual says to clean them. If you plan to store the unit between uses, check the listing's weight and the storage space needed for its accessories.

Map the layout to your meals

If you often cook two things at once, check whether separate baskets and their timing controls suit that routine. If one larger cooking area matters more, compare the listed basket dimensions. If you want oven modes or accessories, confirm exactly what the current listing and manual include.
