Living with the machine · 6 min read

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

By ShopNow Reviews editorial desk Published 2026-11-20 Updated 2026-11-20 How this was written

The most common reason an air fryer ends up in a cupboard is not that it cooks badly. It is that it is annoying to clean and there is nowhere to put it.

This is a checklist for judging both, before you buy, using measurements you can take in your own kitchen in about ten minutes.

Part one: measure the counter before you read a single spec

  1. Width under the upper cabinets. Measure the clear height between the counter and the bottom of the cabinet. Oven-style machines need clearance above them for the door and for vented steam.
  2. Forward clearance. A pulled-out drawer travels toward you. Mark that distance with tape on the counter, then see whether you can still stand there.
  3. Side clearance. Machines vent hot air. If a unit sits against a wall or a fridge, check the manufacturer's stated clearance.
  4. Storage route. If the plan is to put it away between uses, measure the cupboard and weigh that against how often you will realistically do it.

The one that people forget

Height plus open-door height, not height alone. A machine can fit under a cabinet and still be unusable there.

Part two: judge the cleaning, honestly

Part three: the questions that separate a keeper from a cupboard dweller

  • Can I take out every part that touches food, without tools?
  • Do the parts fit in my sink, or do they only fit in the dishwasher?
  • Does the manual describe a drip tray or a crumb tray? If not, where does the grease go?
  • Is the window on the drawer, or on the door, and can I see in without opening it?
  • How many separate pieces do I wash after a single cook?

What the count of parts actually predicts

A dual-basket machine has two drawers, two crisper plates, and often a divider. An oven-style machine has a rack, a tray, a door, and a cavity. Neither is wrong. They simply cost different amounts of attention, and the one you will keep is the one whose attention cost you will actually pay.

If you are choosing between the two shapes, our basket versus oven guide covers the cooking side of the same decision. The capacity checklist covers the size question that sits between them.

What is not in this guide

No cleaning times, no measured noise levels and no measured counter footprints. We have not tested these machines, so any minute count or decibel figure here would be fabricated.
