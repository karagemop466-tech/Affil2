Sizing and capacity · 6 min read

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

By ShopNow Reviews editorial desk Published 2026-11-20 Updated 2026-11-20 How this was written

Every manufacturer quotes quarts. Almost none of them tell you how much food actually fits in a single layer. That is the number your cooking depends on.

Quart capacity is a volume measurement of the chamber. It tells you how much space exists, not how much food will crisp. Two machines can share a capacity figure and produce very different results, because browning depends on hot air reaching the surface of the food.

Four numbers that describe capacity honestly

What each number tells you, and how to read it
NumberWhat it measuresHow to use it
Quart capacityChamber volumeUse it to compare machines in the same shape. Do not compare a basket to an oven cavity with it.
Basket depthHow deep the food can pileDeep chambers tempt you to fill them, which traps steam. Ask how deep, then plan a single layer anyway.
Rack areaUsable flat surfaceOn oven-style units this decides how many pieces you can spread out at once.
Number of zonesIndependent chambersTwo zones let you cook at two temperatures. One zone makes you sequence your cooking.

The single-layer rule, and why it overrides the quart figure

Air frying works by moving hot air over a surface. Food touching food protects the contact points, so those areas steam instead of crisping. A tightly packed 8-quart basket can produce softer results than a half-full 4-quart basket.

Practically, this means capacity is a ceiling, not a promise. Choose the size that fits a single layer of what you actually cook, then accept that you may need two rounds for a crowd.

How to translate capacity into meals

  1. Name your largest regular cook. A whole chicken? A tray of vegetables? Four portions of fries?
  2. Estimate that food as a single layer on a flat surface. If it cannot spread out, the machine is too small for that meal.
  3. Multiply by how often you cook it. Occasional big meals do not justify a machine that dominates the counter every day.
  4. Check the shape against your storage. A machine that does not fit a cabinet has to earn permanent counter space.

On the specific machines on our shortlist

Our published capacity figures are the manufacturer's own: 6 quarts split into two 3-quart baskets on the Chefman dual-basket, a single 8-quart drawer on the Chefman TurboFry, and a 26-quart cavity on the Emeril Lagasse French-door oven. Those figures describe chamber volume. Use the four numbers above to decide what they mean for the food you cook.

What we will not tell you

We have not measured how much food fits in any of these machines, because we have not had them in a kitchen. Any figure we published for usable food volume would be invented. We will add it only after a measured test with a stated method.
