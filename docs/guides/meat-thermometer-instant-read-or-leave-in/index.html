Instant-read or leave-in thermometer: which job are you doing?

By ShopNow Reviews editorial desk Published 2026-11-20 Updated 2026-11-20 How this was written

A thermometer is the cheapest upgrade in the kitchen, and the one most often bought on the wrong criterion. Speed is easy to market. Accuracy after six months is what you actually need.

Instant-read versus leave-in, in one paragraph

An instant-read thermometer is a probe you insert, read, and remove. It gives you a single number at a single moment, so it works for anything you can open the oven to check. A leave-in thermometer stays in the food while it cooks and reports continuously, which is useful for long cooks where you do not want to hover, and for anything where opening the door changes the result.

The specification that matters most is the accuracy claim

Manufacturers publish an accuracy figure such as plus-or-minus 0.5 degrees Fahrenheit. Read it as a claim that needs context, not a measurement you can rely on. Ask two things: over what range is that figure stated, and how is it checked after the probe has been washed repeatedly?

Ice-point check you can do yourself

Fill a glass with crushed ice and a little water, stir, and let it sit for a few minutes. Insert the probe without touching the glass. A thermometer reading close to 32 F (0 C) in that bath is behaving sanely. This is the standard way to sanity-check any probe, and it needs no equipment beyond ice.

Many probes also have a stated calibration function in the manual. Use the manufacturer's procedure rather than an invented one.

Four things to check that are not on the marketing page

  • Probe diameter. A thin probe leaves a smaller hole in the meat and reads faster. Ask for the diameter in millimetres.
  • Ambient rating. If a probe is meant to stay in an oven, it needs an ambient temperature rating separate from the food temperature range.
  • Water resistance. A probe you cannot wash properly is a probe you will not trust. Look for a stated IP rating.
  • Battery and charging. Replaceable batteries are simpler. Rechargeable probes need a charging routine that survives your actual habits.

The temperatures are not negotiable

Whatever you buy, doneness targets come from the USDA, not from us. The published safe minimum internal temperatures are poultry at 165 F (73.9 C), ground meats at 160 F (71.1 C), whole-muscle steaks, chops and roasts at 145 F (62.8 C) with a three-minute rest, fish and shellfish at 145 F (62.8 C), and leftovers and casseroles at 165 F (73.9 C). We link the official chart on each thermometer page so you can check it yourself rather than taking our word for it.
