Buying process · 5 min read

Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

By ShopNow Reviews editorial desk Published 2026-11-20 Updated 2026-11-20 How this was written

Multi-function appliances are sold on function count. They are kept or returned on five other things. Here are the questions to ask before the box arrives.

1. Which single function do I need most, and does this machine do it without a workaround?

A machine with twelve presets and a weak primary function is worse than a machine with three presets and a strong primary function. Identify the one thing you will do weekly, then read the specification for that thing specifically.

2. How many pieces come out to be washed?

Count the parts that touch food: baskets, crisper plates, racks, trays, dividers. Then ask where each one goes in your sink. Function count goes up, part count goes up with it.

3. What is the counter cost, including the door?

Height alone is not the constraint. Add the space the door or drawer needs when open, and the clearance the manual asks for around vents. A machine that has to be turned sideways to open is not usable where it sits.

4. What does the app or the connected part actually require?

Connected thermometers and app-driven appliances add a dependency. Ask what happens without the phone: does it still cook, does it still display temperature, and what is the process for replacing a probe or a battery?

5. What is the return and support route if the primary function disappoints?

Check the stated warranty period and who honours it. This is the part people skip, and it is the part that decides whether a disappointing machine becomes a problem or a footnote.

On food safety, briefly

If you are buying a thermometer to go with the appliance, the target temperatures are not a matter of taste. The USDA publishes a safe minimum internal temperature chart: poultry to 165 F, ground meats to 160 F, whole-muscle steaks, chops and roasts to 145 F with a three-minute rest, and fish to 145 F. We link that chart directly on our thermometer pages rather than restating numbers from memory.

Where to go next

If the appliance question is still open, start with basket or oven-style. If the size question is open, go to the capacity checklist. If the concern is upkeep, the cleanup and counter-space guide is the one to read.
