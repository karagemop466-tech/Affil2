The platform behind the publication, written down before it is built

A design for the machinery that would run this site at ten times the volume without turning it into a list of scraped product links. Nothing on this page is a claim that the machinery exists. It is the specification, the boundaries, and the gates — so that when any part of it gets built, it gets built the way it was described here.

Read this first

This is a concept, not a product. The columns below separate what runs in this repository today from what is designed but not built. A reader will never see the difference, because everything a reader sees is published by a person. The split is here for the operator: it is the honest way to write down an automation plan without implying that automation already exists.

The pipeline

Every stage has a machine doing the mechanical work and a person doing the judgement. The rule that makes it safe is that a stage may never advance on its own: each one ends in a gate that a human clears.

Seven stages. No stage is skipped, and no stage publishes itself.

The six modules

Each module lists what it would do, what it is allowed to touch, and the blocker that stops it. “Blocked” means the design is complete but the input is not available yet.

The automation boundary

Machines may

  • Fetch a public page and record the text that was on it, with a date.
  • Re-render pages, tables, the sitemap and the feed from the data.
  • Run the publishing checks and stop a deploy that fails one.
  • Count the five events at a first-party endpoint we run.
  • Diff two captures of the same listing and report the difference.
  • Propose an outline, a headline or a topic, for a person to accept or reject.

Machines may never

  • Publish a page without a person reading it first.
  • Write a price, a star rating, a review count or a commission rate that was not read from an approved source.
  • Fill an intended ASIN, or merge it with an observed redirect target.
  • Claim a supplied short link is valid or invalid. That question belongs to the owner and to Associates support.
  • Estimate a conversion rate, a revenue figure or a popularity rank.
  • Override a failing compliance check to make a deploy go through.

Why it is written down at all

A design that only exists in the operator's head cannot be reviewed, and a review that cannot be repeated is not a review. This page exists so that the next person — human or machine — can see exactly what the automation is supposed to do, what it is forbidden to do, and which single unresolved item is standing between the design and reality for each module.

Almost every row above is waiting on the same small number of things: an enrolled Associates account (FLG-004, Q-02, Q-11), a deployed collector (FLG-033), and an owner decision about whether the automation roadmap should be authorised at all (SG-12). Those are in Open questions and Suggestions, and they are deliberately not resolved by anything on this page.

What we will NOT do

  • Show hardcoded prices or star ratings.
  • Claim “best”, popularity, or search volume without evidence.
  • Auto-publish AI content without human review.
  • Merge intended and observed ASIN fields.
  • Scale Special Links before the one-per-product test passes.
  • Let an automated job publish without passing the same gate a person would.
