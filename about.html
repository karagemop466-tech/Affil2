About this site

An independent kitchen-appliance buying guide covering five specific products. It is small on purpose: five products documented properly beat five hundred documented by scraping.

What this is

A publication-style affiliate site. When a reader buys through one of our tagged Amazon links, we may earn a commission. That is the business model, it is disclosed on every page, and no recommendation here is influenced by which product pays more — partly because no commission rate for any of these products has been confirmed, and this site does not publish one.

Who writes it

The byline you see on every guide and product page is The Kitchen Desk — the name this publication uses for the desk that reads listings, records values with their dates, and writes the copy. It is a role byline, deliberately. There is no named human author of record yet, and rather than invent a person to carry a byline, the site says so and keeps the item open (FLG-033) until the owner names someone. When a name exists it replaces the desk name on the same line, in the same place, with the same dated verification stamp.

What the desk does: opens the official listing, reads each value off the page, records it with a date and a URL, and raises a flag whenever two parts of the same page disagree. The process is written out on the How we choose page.

What the desk has not done: cooked with any of these appliances, measured any of them, or seen a certification document. No page on this site claims otherwise.

Why only five products

Because those five are the ones this site covers. There is no pretence of covering the market, no "top ten" list, and no ranking against products that were never examined. Where a guide would normally say "consider alternatives", it says instead that the site only covers these five.

Corrections

Listings change. If a value here no longer matches the official listing, that is a correction to be made, not an argument to be had. Every flag on the Flagged for Review page has a source link and a suggested resolution, and corrections are dated rather than quietly applied.

How this content was made

Google's guidance on people-first content asks a publisher to say who made a page, how it was made, and to keep it factually accurate. In answer, in plain terms:

  • How: every figure on this site was taken by reading an official source — an Amazon listing's own title, bullets or Product information tables, or a USDA/FTC/Google page — then written up against that dated record. Nothing was scraped into a template, and no number here is remembered rather than looked up.
  • What it is not: a test report. No product was bought, cooked with, weighed or calibrated for this site, and no page implies otherwise.
  • Why the byline is a desk and not a person: a fabricated name, headshot or credential is what Google calls deceptive authorship information. A role byline carries the same accountability without inventing a biography. The named editor of record is an open item, not a styling choice.
  • Source for those standards: Google Search Central — Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content (S25 in the source register).

How a page is signed

Every guide and every product page carries the same three-part stamp under its headline: who wrote it (The Kitchen Desk), what the numbers were checked against, and the date they were read. A read time on a guide is measured from the copy on the page at build time, never typed in by hand, because a promise to a reader should be derived from the thing being promised.

The site's internal register codes — the FLG- numbers that track every conflict and every blank — live on this page, on the Flagged for Review page and in the audit tables at the bottom of each product page. They are deliberately kept out of buyer-facing sentences: a shopper comparing two air fryers should read “the listing states two different wattages”, not a ticket number. Everything a code refers to is still one click away.

Imagery and trademarks

Two kinds of editorial imagery appear on this site, and both are explicitly labelled. The photographs on the storefront pages were generated for this site: they show a kitchen and appliances of no particular make, and every frame is captioned on the page so nobody mistakes it for the specific product reviewed (FLG-036). Alongside them, original unbranded SVG form-factor schematics stored in assets/illustrations/ illustrate the three appliance shapes covered here. No Amazon product photography is used anywhere (FLG-007).

Contact and change requests

There is no contact form and no email capture on this site. Change requests go through the Flagged for Review page, which lists every open item with its ID, and through the Decision Center, where proposals are ranked before anything is built.
